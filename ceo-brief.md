# ISO/IEC 27001 as a financial decision for the CEO

> A 30-minute briefing for the executive. What it costs, what it pays back, when to start and when you can wait.

By 2026, mid-market and enterprise B2B are framing ISO 27001 differently. The old debate was "do we certify or not." The new one is "how narrow do we scope it and when do we start."

This file helps a CEO make the call and defend it to the board. All numbers are in USD, drawn from primary industry sources: [IBM Cost of a Data Breach Report](https://www.ibm.com/reports/data-breach) and aggregate reports from ISMS.online, Secureframe and Advisera. Local rates in Belarus and Kazakhstan can be 30–60% lower.

## What the data says

If you have 60 seconds, here's the headline:

- **43%** of certified companies report sales growth ([BSI Group survey of 645 organisations](https://www.isms.online/iso-27001/certification/is-it-worth-it/), aggregated via ISMS.online).
- Most see positive ROI **within 12 months** (ISMS.online, based on BSI data).
- **73%** rate the cost of certification as either fully justified by the benefits or comparable to similar standards ([Secureframe ROI analysis](https://secureframe.com/blog/is-iso-27001-worth-it)).
- Per the [IBM *Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025*](https://www.ibm.com/reports/data-breach), the global average breach cost fell to **USD 4.44 million** (from USD 4.88M a year earlier). Encryption alone saves ~**USD 208K per breach**; organisations with a mature ISMS see ~**USD 1.2M lower** breach costs than non-certified peers (ISMS.online aggregate of IBM 2024); extensive AI-defence use adds up to **USD 1.9M** in savings.
- Cyber-insurance premiums drop **15–25%** with a certificate in place.

## What it costs

### Understanding the budget

Certification isn't a one-time expense. It's a 3-year cycle: Stage 1 plus Stage 2 in year 1, a surveillance audit in year 2, another in year 3, and recertification at the end of year 3.

**Why there's no price list.** No universal price exists. The five factors below (company size, security maturity, scope width, geography, CB – Certification Body – choice) push identical-looking businesses into a 3–5x range. Any "average" number would mislead you. Better to walk through the inputs first, then quote a number.

### What drives the final budget

Five factors decide where in the range your project lands:

- **Security maturity going in.** If MFA, EDR, vulnerability scanning and basic policies are already in place, consulting drops by 30–50%.
- **Scope.** One product vs. the whole company – a 2–3x difference.
- **CB choice.** Regional vs. top-tier international – another 30–50% gap.
- **Tooling.** GRC platforms (Vanta, Drata, Sprinto) save 40–60% of FTE hours.
- **Geography.** Multi-region adds 30–100% in time and cost.

### What makes up the total

- External consulting or fractional expert.
- GRC tooling (annual subscription).
- Certification audit: Stage 1, Stage 2, two surveillance audits, recertification.
- Internal FTE hours.
- Other: gap analysis, technical tuning.

### Where you can save

**Narrow scope from the start.** Certify one product or one business unit first. Expand at next year's surveillance audit. Biggest lever on TCO – a 2–3x saving.

**Mature security practice before you start.** If MFA, EDR, vulnerability scanning and basic policies are already in place, consulting costs drop 30–50%.

**Local CB.** BelGISS in Belarus, NCA-accredited bodies in Kazakhstan. Usually 30–50% cheaper than international CBs, with comparable recognition inside local jurisdictions. If your priority is EU or UK markets, use an international CB with IAF MLA recognition.

**GRC tooling instead of manual work.** Vanta, Drata, Sprinto and Secureframe automate evidence collection – 40–60% off FTE hours.

## What it pays back

### Access to B2B tenders

Enterprise procurement in IT, healthcare, finance and government treats the ISO 27001 certificate as a **knockout criterion** at pre-qualification. Without it you don't make the shortlist.

This is especially true for:

- Microsoft SSPA (Supplier Security and Privacy Assurance) – mandatory for Microsoft suppliers.
- AWS Marketplace, Google Cloud Marketplace and Azure Marketplace premium tiers.
- Government contracts in the EU, UK and UAE.
- Financial institutions under DORA – ISO 27001 is increasingly required in practice as evidence of adequate ICT third-party risk management (DORA Art. 28–30). Certification isn't formally mandatory, but partner banks and financial-institution procurement treat it as the baseline.

### Shorter sales cycle

Certified companies typically get a shorter security questionnaire from enterprise buyers. Sometimes the questionnaire is waived entirely – the certificate is enough.

The effect:

- Sales cycle shortens by **4–12 weeks** per enterprise deal.
- Industry surveys (ISMS.online, Secureframe) report a substantial drop in deals "lost on compliance grounds" – estimates range roughly from 45% to 80% depending on the source.

### Lower insurance premiums

Cyber liability insurance runs mid-market organisations tens of thousands of dollars a year. An ISO 27001 certificate cuts premiums by **15–25%**. Some insurers now **refuse coverage** without one.

Over the 3-year certification cycle, insurance savings alone often cover a large portion of the certification project cost.

### Lower cost per incident

When an incident does happen (and it happens everywhere), a certified company:

- Responds 3–5x faster thanks to a working incident management process.
- Holds documented evidence of appropriate technical and organisational measures, reducing regulatory fines and legal exposure.
- Protects reputation through a documented response, not chaotic press releases.

Per IBM *Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025*, the global average breach cost fell to **USD 4.44M**. Organisations with a mature ISMS see approximately **USD 1.2M lower** breach costs than non-certified peers (ISMS.online aggregate on IBM 2024 data). Encryption alone saves ~**USD 208K per breach**; extensive use of AI defence tools adds up to **USD 1.9M** in savings.

### The "after-incident" premium

Companies that invest in security only after the first serious incident spend on average **50% more** than those who invested upfront. That's the reactivity premium – on top of the direct losses from the incident itself.

## Indirect benefits

**Regulatory confidence.** One framework that covers GDPR, NIS2, DORA, UK GDPR, KVKK, UAE PDPL, and Belarusian and Kazakh laws. See [comparison-regulations.md](comparison-regulations.md). Lower compliance costs and faster reaction time to new requirements – a new law gets folded in over days, not months.

**Operational maturity.** Implementation gives you an asset inventory (often for the first time), a risk register, a working incident management process, and formalised supplier security. That cuts operational costs on rework and cleanup after configuration mistakes.

**M&A premium.** In M&A due diligence, certification:

- Cuts security DD by 50–70%.
- Reduces the cyber-risk discount in company valuation.
- Lifts the multiplier in cyber-sensitive sectors (SaaS, fintech, healthcare).

**Hiring and retention.** Strong security professionals prefer mature ISMS environments. 27001 certification is a signal to the talent market.

## When to start now

Certification pays for itself if any of these signals are present:

- Current or upcoming enterprise deals require the certificate.
- You're in a regulated industry – finance, healthcare, critical infrastructure.
- Expansion into new jurisdictions – EU, UK, UAE – with regulatory requirements.
- You had an incident or near-miss and need organisational discipline.
- Preparation for a funding round or M&A in the next 12–24 months.
- Competitors in your segment are already certified and use it in sales.

---

## When you can wait

Don't rush if:

- You're a small business with no enterprise clients and no regulatory requirements.
- Your security practice isn't even basic yet – you can't certify holes.
- Cash flow is unstable and the certification investment would strain the budget.

This isn't "never do it." It's "wait until certification actually pays off."

## Alternatives to 27001

Sometimes a lighter option is a better fit.

**SOC 2 Type 1 or Type 2.** Faster and cheaper for the US market. If most of your clients are American, start with SOC 2.

**Cyber Essentials Plus (UK).** A pre-tender requirement for UK government contracts. Implementation in 2–6 weeks.

**NESA Light tier (UAE).** For non-critical sectors in the Emirates.

**Self-attestation against 27001.** No certificate, but a "trust signal" for marketing. Won't get you through formal tenders, but cuts the security questionnaire load.

---

## Narrow scope or wide scope

If you've decided to start, one universal rule: **start narrow**.

Certify one product or one business unit. Expand later through a scope change at the next surveillance audit.

What this buys you:

- Initial costs 2–3x lower.
- Time-to-certification of 4–8 months instead of 12–18.
- Early commercial payoff – the certificate is usable in sales in year 1.
- A chance to learn at small scale before you expand.

The mistake that sinks projects: trying to cover the whole company on the first attempt. The team drowns in volume, the project stretches to 18–24 months, and often never makes it to Stage 2.

If you've decided to start:

1. Download [ISO/IEC 27000:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/73906.html) – the free vocabulary standard. 50 pages.

4. Build the team internally. Minimum: one FTE leading the project.
5. Decide on consulting. Without an experienced CISO, going it alone takes 2–3x longer.
